What you are actually watching

Thirty-two fruit flies are in that room, sixteen couples. Each one has a whole brain: 138,639 neurons and 15,091,983 synapses, wired exactly the way they are wired in a real Drosophila melanogaster, from the FlyWire connectome. They run as leaky integrate-and-fire neurons on one GPU, in real electrical time, 0.1 ms per step.

Nothing in that room is scripted. When someone buys on the curve, the buy is turned into a real sensory stimulus and injected into specific neurons — the same ones a real fly fires when it is touched, when it tastes sugar, when a shadow passes overhead. The brains answer on their own.

How a buy becomes sex

A buy raises the heat of the coldest couples first, so the wave travels across the room instead of always cooking the same pair. Every thrust of every couple fires JO — the Johnston's organ, 209 mechanosensory neurons, the fly's sense of touch and vibration — in both brains of that couple, plus pC1 (10 neurons, female receptivity) in hers. Every fourth thrust dumps PAM dopamine, 307 neurons, into both. A big sell fires DNp13 + oviDN in the hottest female and she throws him off.

Why thirty-two brains fit on one card

The expensive part of a connectome is the synapse matrix, and it is the same matrix for every fly. So the thirty-two brains share one copy of it and each fly is a row of the state tensor: 27 MB of membrane state each instead of 1.3 GB. Measured on the RTX 4090 that runs this: one brain alone does 2,328 steps per second, thirty-two do about 127 each, in 1.03 GB.

What is honest here

The brains are real and independent: stimulate fly 3 and only fly 3 fires. The bodies are the same anatomical model (NeuroMechFly v2) drawn thirty-two times, each reading the pose stream at its own delay, with its couple's own rhythm on top — so the legs and wings are real fly kinematics, not thirty-two separate physics simulations.

Learning is off. Hebbian plasticity would change the shared synapse matrix, which would mean the swarm learning together instead of each fly learning alone, and we are not going to call that individual learning.

Stimulus length is measured in brain time, and brain time runs about ninety times slower than the clock. That is why a touch here lasts a millisecond and not half a second: otherwise a single thrust would still be ringing forty seconds later.

If the badge at the top says no signal, the GPU is off and you are looking at a still room. We do not fake the movement.
